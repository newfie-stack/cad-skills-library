# Current-fact and scenario checks

Optional depth for war-room decisions that depend on fast-changing facts, contested reports, or uncertain futures. This adds evidence discipline to the five-lens process; it never replaces a role or skips cross-review.

## Verify what matters now

- Identify the few claims that could change the decision. Verify them with current primary sources where available.
- Record source, publication/update time, event time if different, and verification time. Distinguish an observed event from a forecast or an interested party's claim.
- Compare genuinely independent sources for contested claims. Multiple reports quoting the same origin are one evidence chain.
- Mark disagreements and information gaps explicitly. Do not resolve them through rhetorical confidence or advisor votes.
- If tools cannot verify current facts, state the stale or unverified basis and recommend only bounded conditional action supported by it.

A compact claim record can use: claim; status (verified / disputed / unverified); source and time; consequence if false; confidence with reason. Confidence concerns the claim's support, not its political popularity or emotional salience.

## Scenarios when they change the decision

Use a small set of materially distinct scenarios, not an exhaustive speculative story. For each, state:

- Conditions required and key uncertain assumptions.
- Observable leading indicators and how they could be checked.
- Decision implication, reversible preparation, and trigger to change course.
- Confidence with its basis; avoid invented numerical probabilities.

Choose a next checkpoint when a relevant indicator should become observable, a dependency resolves, or a real deadline approaches. Do not default to fixed 24-hour or week-long windows without justification. Distinguish “we should check later” from an actually scheduled or authorized monitoring task.

## Domain boundaries

Keep political, geopolitical, conflict, and crisis analysis neutral, civilian, and non-operational. Discuss public facts, uncertainty, humanitarian effects, civilian safety, and high-level decision implications. Do not provide targeting, tactical assistance, operational violence planning, or evasion guidance. Apply the host's safety rules and relevant professional boundaries in every domain.

Examples, past scenarios, and geopolitical narratives are not evidence of present conditions. No factual claim from a source example is carried into a live decision without verification. The name “war room” grants no special permissions and does not imply command authority.
